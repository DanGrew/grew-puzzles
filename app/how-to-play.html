How to play

Find every word in the list. The puzzle's done when they're all found: here, CAT, DOG and HEN.

Words run in eight directions: across, down, diagonally, and backwards in any of them. OWL here reads up and to the left, on a diagonal and backwards.

Words can cross, sharing a letter. COW and CAT share their C, and a shared letter gets a circle.

A word inside another word doesn't count. The PIG inside PIGLET isn't the one you're after: PIG is hidden on its own.

To mark a word, tap its first letter, then its last. Tap the F, then the X. Either end works.

A found word is crossed off the list. FOX is found, so it's crossed off.

Hunt the rarest letter first

When a word has a letter the grid hardly uses, like Z, Q, X or J.

Double letters

When a word has the same letter twice in a row, like the TT in KITTEN.

A word's first and last letter together

When the grid is full of a word's letters, look for its first and last, the word's length apart.

One direction at a time

When a word just won't turn up. Read the grid one way only, right to left, say.

A long word before a short one

When the list has long words and short ones. A long word has far fewer places it can fit.

Vanilla wordsearches

A nice calming wordsearch. No tricks, no challenges; just a normal puzzle: find the words in the list. Words can run in any direction, including diagonally. Of course, they can overlap, but rest assured that words within words (like 'grape' within 'grapefruit') appear as two separate words in the grid.

Missing words wordsearches

This is the first type of challenge! The word list includes one or more words that aren't in the puzzle… You might have been looking so hard for a word that isn't actually in the puzzle! What fun!

Repeating words wordsearches

The next challenge doesn't involve any trickery, but it does test your focus! The puzzle contains only one word… but lots of it. What starts off as a novelty might leave you seeing the word 'sheep' in your sleep!

Wildcard wordsearches

Back to tricky stuff: wildcards are letters in the puzzle hidden behind a '?'. Each one stands for a single letter, which could be any letter, and each wildcard can be a different one. Basically, we've just hidden letters to make it harder for you!

Saga wordsearches

No more rules, just lots of words! These puzzles have a huge list of words split across several grids. And you guessed it, you have no idea which words are in which grid!

Mirra?e wordsearches

The ultimate challenge: every trick at once! Two puzzle grids, a word list with words that aren't really there, '?' wildcards hiding letters, and one word hidden five times across both grids. Like any good mirage, not everything you see is real… Good luck!

Kids wordsearches

Perfect for little puzzlers! A small grid with big letters, and every word in the list written in capitals, just like in the grid. Words only go forwards: left to right, top to bottom, or diagonally down to the right. No backwards words, no tricks, just lots of finding fun!

Get from the start to the end. Find the one way through, from the shaded start square to End.

Move with the control pad. One press of ↓ runs the trail down the corridor and round its bends, and stops at the first junction. The arrow keys work too. A run stops at every junction, dead end and thing on the checklist, so every choice is yours.

Walls can't be crossed. The circled square is right beside the trail, but a wall is in the way, so the pad greys out →. The trail has to go the long way round (dashed).

Go back over the trail. ↑ ran the trail into a dead end (faded). Back takes it back one run, to the junction it left. Pressing the way it came in does the same. Anything picked up stays ticked.

Guides are on the right path. Guides sit only on the one route from the start to the end. A run stops on each one, and its box ticks on the checklist.

Fill in dead ends

When you can see the whole maze, or you've printed it.

Work back from the end

When the start has lots of choices, but the end looks quiet.

Use a guide as a waypoint

When there's a guide in the maze.

Vanilla mazes

A nice calming maze. Find your way from the start to the end, and pick up the guides as you go. They're all on the right path, so if you've found one, you're heading the right way!

Collectibles mazes

There's treasure off the beaten track! Collectibles are hidden down the side paths, never on the way to the end. Find every one, then make your way out.

Code Breaker mazes

Six exits, but only one is right! Find A, B and C, hidden down side paths. Follow the one true route from the start, and the order you pass their turnings is the code. That's your exit! Want to finish the whole maze? Find all six exits: the right one gets a ✓ and the rest get a ✗.

Keys mazes

Some of the maze is locked! Bump into a locked zone and it'll show you the number of the key it needs. That key is somewhere outside the zone. Find it and the zone opens up. Pick up every key to finish!

Keylecticodes mazes

Everything at once! Guides, collectibles, keys and zones, A, B and C and six exits, all in one maze. Good luck!

Find every mine. Mark them all and the board is solved: here, all four. Squares marked no mine don't count toward it.

A number counts the mines in the eight squares around it. This 3 has three mines in the eight squares around it, inside the box.

A numbered square is never a mine. The 2's two mines are in the blank squares around it. The 1 beside it is a number, so it's never one of them, and it can't be marked.

Edges and corners have fewer squares around them. A number in a corner has only three squares around it, and one on an edge has five. Each 1 here has its one mine inside its box.

Tap a square once to mark it no mine, again to mark it a mine, and again to clear it. A square marked no mine turns grey. The Mines card beside the board says the same.

Tap a number to fill in around it. This 2 has both its mines marked, so its other blank squares fill in as no mine. If a number's blank squares must all be mines, they fill in as mines. Otherwise nothing happens. It goes by your own marks, so a wrong mark carries in.

Check shows your mistakes. It rings in red every mark that's wrong: here, a mine where there isn't one, and no mine on a mine. The line under it says how many, and the rings go at your next tap. Blank squares are never checked.

Undo takes back a tap. Here it takes back the 2's fill, all in one go. Press it again to go further back. There's no Redo.

You never need to guess, except on a Canaries board. Every board has exactly one answer, and on all but a Canaries board its numbers alone lead to it. This one is solved from its six numbers.

A number already met

When a number already has all its mines marked.

A number whose blank squares must all be mines

When a number is missing as many mines as it has blank squares.

1-1 against an edge

When two 1s sit side by side, the first against the board's edge.

1-2-1 along a row

When a 1, a 2 and a 1 sit side by side, with a row of blank squares along them.

1-2-2-1 along a row

When a 1, two 2s and a 1 sit side by side, with a row of blank squares along them.

Taking one number's mines away from its neighbour's

When two numbers side by side share some of their squares.

Vanilla minesweeper

No luck needed, just a little thinking! Some squares show a number. Each number counts the mines in the eight squares around it, and a square with a number is never a mine. Tap a square once to mark it safe, and it turns grey, again to mark a mine, and again to clear it. In a hurry? Tap a number. If all its mines are marked, its other squares are marked safe. If its empty squares must all be mines, they're marked for you. Every board can be solved by logic alone, so you'll never have to guess. Mark every mine to finish!

Reveal minesweeper

Every square starts covered, apart from a little area to get you going. Each number counts the mines in the eight squares around it. Pick Open or Mark mine under the board, then tap a square. A right-click or a long press always marks a mine. Open a mine and it's Kaboom! But you can always try again. Tap a number with all its mines marked, and its other squares open for you. Every board can be solved by logic alone, so you'll never have to guess. Open every safe square to finish!

Canaries minesweeper

Some boards can't be solved by logic alone, so you get canaries to help! Each tile says how many. Pick Canary and tap a square. If it's safe, it opens. If it's a mine, you lose the canary but not the game: the mine stays shown, and you carry on. You get exactly one canary for each guess the board needs. Open every safe square to finish!

Keys minesweeper

Some zones of the board are padlocked in their own colour, and you can't open them yet. Each zone has a key hidden under one of the board's squares. Open that square and the key pops up, the zone's padlocks drop, and in you go. Every board can be solved by logic alone, so you'll never have to guess. Open every safe square to finish!

Limited Flags minesweeper

You only have so many flags! The row of flags under the board shows how many you've got left. Mark a mine with none left, and your oldest flag moves to the new square. Undo puts it back. Every board can be solved by logic alone, so you'll never have to guess. Open every safe square to finish!

Fit every word into the shape. Every word in the list fills one slot, across or down. Once each is in its own slot, the shape says Solved.

Words cross where they share a letter. Where two slots cross they share a square, so both words share its letter: Apple and Pear share this P.

One word starts in place. The starting word is filled in for you, shaded and crossed off the list. It can't be moved or taken out.

Tap a square to pick a slot. The first tap picks the slot across, and the line over the words says which. Where two slots cross, tap again for the slot down, and again to go back across.

Then tap a word to put it in. While a slot is picked, words of other lengths dim. Tap a word that long and it goes in and is crossed off.

A word that clashes won't go in. If a word disagrees with letters already in the slot, the clashing squares turn red and it stays out. Lime won't fit 2 down: its first square is Apple's P.

Tap a placed word to take it out. Letters another word shares stay: Pear comes out, but Apple's P and Green's R stay.

Moving a word to another slot. With a slot picked, tapping a crossed-off word moves it there. Pear was in 6 across: pick 2 down, tap Pear, and it moves.

No guessing needed. Every shape has exactly one answer, and the letters already in lead to it. A wrong word is never pointed out.

The only word of its length

A crossing letter that leaves one word that fits

When a letter already in a slot rules out all but one word its length.

A word that fits nowhere else

When no slot has just one word that fits, turn it round: find the word with just one slot.

Working out from the starting word

When you're stuck at the start. The starting word's letters are the first clues.

Vanilla criss-crosses

A list of words and an empty shape: can you fit them all in? One word is already in place to get you started. Words cross where they share a letter, so every word you place gives you letters for the next. Tap a slot, then tap a word to put it in. If it doesn't fit the letters already there, it won't go in. Changed your mind? Tap a word you've placed to take it back out. Every puzzle can be solved by logic alone, so you'll never have to guess. Fill every slot to finish!

Shade squares to reveal a picture. Every board hides one. Shade exactly its squares and the board is solved: the numbers and lines melt away, and the title becomes the picture's name.

A number counts the shaded squares in its block of nine. Its block is its own square and the eight around it. This 6 has six shaded and three left blank.

A numbered square can be shaded too. It taps like any other square. Shaded, its number shows white.

A 0 and a 9 come filled in. Before your first tap, a 0's whole block is left blank and a 9's is shaded, the number's own square too. They tap like any other square, but Undo never takes them back.

Tap a square once to leave it blank, again to shade it, and again to clear it. A square left blank turns pale grey. The Squares card beside the board counts the squares shaded.

Right-click a number to fill in its block. On a touch screen, pick Fill under the board and tap it. If its clear squares are just what it still needs, they're shaded. If its count is already met, they're left blank. Otherwise nothing happens. Here the 6 needs all six; then the 1 has its one, so the rest are left blank.

Check rings your mistakes. It rings in red every square shaded that isn't in the picture, and every square left blank that is. The line under it says how many. A clear square is never checked.

Undo takes back a tap. A fill comes back as one: here the 1's five blank squares clear again together. Press it again to go further back.

You never need to guess. Every board has exactly one picture, and its numbers lead to it one sure step at a time. This one is solved from the board as it opens.

Vanilla mosaics

Shade the squares to reveal a hidden picture! Each number counts the shaded squares in its 3×3 block: the square itself and the eight around it. A square with a number can be shaded too. Tap a square once to mark it blank, again to shade it, and again to clear it. Every board can be solved by logic alone, so you'll never have to guess. Shade the whole picture to finish!
